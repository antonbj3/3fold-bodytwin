## 2. The assigned wiring is executed and it fails at the file's own wound shear

With the assigned ("single") wiring at `gamma_w = GAMMA_WOUND_REPRESENTATIVE = 1500 s^-1`
— the **only** representative wound shear the supplied file itself declares — the model
**never closes** (`closure_time_min = inf`, `N_CRIT_EMERGENT = inf`). Six of the twenty supplied
gates flip to FAIL there. Diagnosis, not a tuning failure: `f_high` is a fraction of the
*high-shear GPIb-VWF / soluble-VWF pathway* (fitted to Ruggeri 2006 "A1 progressively disaggregates
above 6000 s^-1" and "stable near 20000 s^-1"), and Savage 1996 says the *other* pathway — direct
αIIbβ3 arrest — is "fully efficient only at wall shear rates below 600-900 s^-1". Multiplying the
**total** on-rate by the high-shear fraction alone therefore drives low-shear adhesion to zero
(`k_on/K_ON0 = 6.0e-5` at 1500 s^-1) and inverts the supplied low-shear biology. **The assigned
change is load-bearing and usable, but wrong as specified.**

## 8. Construction 2: wall shear as a STATE, not an argument (the obstacle from the first pass)

§1–§7 leave one thing obviously wrong: `gamma_w` is a *constant the caller supplies*, but in a real
vessel the wall shear is produced by the very flow the plug is trying to stop. For Poiseuille flow,
`Q = π ΔP r⁴/(8 μ L)` and therefore `gamma_w = 4Q/(π r³) = ΔP·r_eff/(2 μ L)` — **linear in lumen
radius**. A growing plug shrinks the lumen and drives the shear it depends on. Changed operation:
`k_on` is evaluated from the state at every Euler step; aggregation is left shear-independent on
purpose (platelet-platelet bridging on the plug surface, and the supplied F2 gate requires `dP/dt`
to have no shear dependence).

**Parity control (matched, asserted not assumed):** with the occlusion coupling switched off
(`phi_max = 0`) the new solver returns bit-identical results to the supplied constant-shear solver
at `gamma = 200` (`2.660000000000002`), `3000` (`inf`) and `20000` (`2.740000000000002` 1/s). The
flow solver therefore reduces *exactly* to the supplied one; every difference below is the coupling.

**The coupling does not widen the gap symmetrically — it moves the trap and changes who survives:**

| gamma_0 (1/s) | constant shear | flow-coupled | mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.62e2 | 2.700 min | 2.680 min | self-amplifying, closes |
| 1.00e3 | **never** | **15.06 min** | escapes *downward* out of the band and seals |
| 1.78e3 – 5.62e3 | never | never | trapped: 100 % of the trajectory inside the band |
| 1.00e4 | **4.86 min** | **never** | grows, decays into the band, stalls at cov = 0.415 |
| 1.78e4 | 2.820 min | 3.360 min (+19 %) | closes, but slower |
| 3.16e4 | 2.680 min | 2.720 min | closes |

Never-closes band: **[1.00e3, 5.62e3] constant → [1.33e3, 1.00e4] flow-coupled**; multiplicative
width **5.62 → 7.50**, lower edge up 1.33x, upper edge up 1.78x.

The structure is a **two-sided feedback with one self-sealing basin**: below the band the feedback
is positive (shear down → `s_int` up → plug grows → shear down) so the plug escapes and seals; above
the band it is negative (shear down → `f_high` down → growth stalls) so the plug falls into the band
and cannot get out; inside the band nothing grows, so the shear never changes. Under the
constant-shear port, vessels at `gamma_0` just above the gap looked safe (4.86 min at 1e4). They are
not: **the constant-shear port systematically over-predicts haemostasis at moderate-to-high shear,
by up to 100 % (finite → never).** That is the load-bearing consequence of making shear a state.

**Vessel map (which vessels a scalpel actually severs).** `mu = 3.5e-3 Pa·s` (whole blood),
`L = 2 mm` (wound-edge span), `gamma_w = ΔP·r/(2 mu L)`; `ΔP` disclosed per case, not measured here:

| vessel | r (um) | ΔP (Pa) | gamma_w (1/s) | in constant band | in flow band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| capillary | 4 | 1000 | 2.86e2 | no | no |
| arteriole 25 um | 12.5 | 3000 | 2.68e3 | **YES** | **YES** |
| arteriole 50 um | 25 | 3000 | 5.36e3 | **YES** | **YES** |
| arteriole 100 um | 50 | 3000 | 1.07e4 | no | no (borderline) |
| small artery 400 um | 200 | 6000 | 8.57e4 | no | no |
| venule 200 um | 100 | 1333 | 9.52e3 | no | **YES** |

2 of 6 classes inside the constant-shear band, **3 of 6 inside the flow-coupled band**; the
25–50 µm arterioles that dominate bleeding in muscle sit at 2.7e3–5.4e3 s^-1, inside both bands, and
the 100 µm arteriole sits within 7 % of the flow-coupled edge, so the `ΔP` assumption alone moves it
across the boundary. **Under this model the dominant bleeding vessels of an incised muscle wound do
not achieve primary haemostasis at N = 250 x10^9/L.** This is a model output about a model, not a
clinical claim.

   are the only mechanism that could make strong flow defeat a growing plug, i.e. the only thing

4. Absolute closure times inherit the supplied file's illustrative `THETA_C = 0.5` coverage scale and

   never-closes bands.
5. `W_INT` (§6) remains the unidentifiable quantity that decides the sign of the mid-shear effect,
   and `mu`/`ΔP` decide which vessels land in the band. None of these has been measured here.
