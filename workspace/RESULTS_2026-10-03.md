## 1. The eye model was compared to real surgical outcomes for the first time

Source: a published dataset of post-operative refraction, DOI `10.6084/m9.figshare.22736687.v1`,
20 held-out rows. Nothing was fitted to them.

| treatment | bias | MAE | RMSE | max error | within 0.25 D | within 0.50 D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| thin lens | **−0.3865 D** | 0.5301 | 0.6591 | 1.2980 | 7/20 | 12/20 |
| thick lens | **+0.1455 D** | 0.4697 | 0.5902 | 1.4350 | 7/20 | 13/20 |
| index-convention control | +0.1643 D | 0.4754 | 0.5951 | 1.4529 | 7/20 | 13/20 |

**Not yet clinically usable, and the error is localised.** The systematic bias is a lens-model artefact:
−0.39 D with a thin lens, +0.15 D with a thick one. The index-convention control reproduces the
thick-lens figure, which rules out a unit or convention artefact.

The cornea side was separately eliminated as the missing piece. Two corneas with **identical** mean
thickness, refractive index field, hydration and surface allocation — all identity errors exactly 0.0 —
differ by **0.3056 D**, in 4 of 4 tested pairs. What is missing is the three-dimensional surface shape
field and a common coordinate registration. A clinical corneal topographer measures exactly that.

**The chain itself is load-bearing.** Halving the molecular charge inventory
(1.2195e-15 → 6.0976e-16 mol/m) moves the ionic swelling pressure by **−489.65 Pa**, the pupil-mean
thickness by 3.54 µm and the refractive power by **−0.4256 D**, with the Strehl ratio falling
0.1227 → 0.1042. A rotationally symmetric cornea of the same mean thickness gives **−1.36e-08 D**, so
the whole effect comes from non-symmetric structure. Numerics: 4.19e-13 D round-off.

**But the sign is not yet predictable.** Across twelve conditions the change ranges from +0.4658 D to
−0.8618 D; five of ten clear the 0.25 D clinical threshold and the sign flips. The span over the
surface-allocation parameter alone is 0.8724 D, which coincides to four decimals with the measured
sufficiency gap. So the effect is clinically large and its direction is undetermined until surface shape
is measured.

## 3. Complement regulation: a threshold with a unit

As the C3b recycling fraction rises to **0.6901**, the growth rate on a self surface crosses zero
(−0.1511 → −0.0019 per minute) and MAC at 60 minutes rises **0.822 → 8.484 nM**.

Discrimination between an activator surface and a self surface is **486.1** without recycling and
**4.969** with it — a factor of **97.8** on one modelling choice. **Published biology supports the low
end** (immediate Bb dissociation supports the recycling product), so the protective margin is roughly
fivefold, not five-hundredfold.

The threshold is **not** certified as load-bearing: the source's own uncertainty on the parameter is
unmeasured, and the gate is recorded as FAIL for that reason.

## 5. Summary statistics are insufficient far more often than they are tested

|---|---|

## 9. The decisions were asked how confident they are, and the answer is that they do not know

Both surgical decisions emitted a point number. Scoring them as probabilities — leave-one-out, event
"lands within 0.5 D", Brier against the cohort rate as the reference forecast — gives:

| decision | n | Brier | reference | skill | calibration | resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| implant power | 20 | 0.2559 | 0.2659 | +0.038 | 0.0929 | 0.0817 |
| toric cylinder | 69 | 0.1384 | 0.1276 | **−0.085** | 0.0228 | **0.0034** |

The toric forecast is worse than quoting the base rate. A resolution of 0.0034 means it barely
distinguishes patients at all, so the per-patient confidence is not a capability we have.

**Why it failed, measured rather than argued.** The uncertainty was modelled as one scale with one
covariate. Applying the graph engine's decorrelation verifier to a stage decomposition in which each
row sums exactly to the error actually observed:

| decision | units | stages | correlation | verdict | margin | bracket at ρ=0 … ρ=1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| implant power | 20 | grid quantisation, chain | 0.026 | UNDECIDABLE | 0.59 | 0.78 … 0.68 |
| toric cylinder | 69 | posterior information, chain | −0.799 | certified, **withdrawn** | 1.18 | 0.66 … 0.47 |

The toric margin lies outside the bracket the module computes for it, which cannot be right. Its two
stages are (measured − population) and (population − outcome), so the population prediction appears in
both with opposite signs and carries 1.285 times the variance of the first stage: the anticorrelation is
induced by the construction and nothing about the errors was measured. Certification withdrawn.

**The general statement.** An additive stage decomposition of a total that was observed only once is not
identifiable — any two stages summing to a fixed observed error share a term by construction, and their
correlation is an artefact of where the split was placed. The implant-power case is exempt only because
grid quantisation is computed from the 0.5 D manufacturing grid rather than from the outcome; its 0.026
is what an honest near-zero looks like beside a constructed −0.80. The toric case needs a second
independent measurement per eye before any margin can be certified, not a different split.

Instrument repeatability is a third error source and is deliberately absent from both matrices: it is
measured over 300 eyes of a different cohort and instrument with zero overlap with the 20 and 89
surgical eyes, so no definition of a unit admits it. It is carried as a declared component with its
scale and population stated, and every number above is also available with it included.

A Hill tail index of 0.45 on the raw implant-power stage errors says that distribution is heavy-tailed,
which is a further reason not to trust any single standard deviation there.

## 10. The toric result confirmed against a second instrument, and what that cost to establish

The decorrelation work ended on the conclusion that a second independent measurement per eye was
needed. It exists: the surgical workbook carries two instruments on the same eyes — IOLMaster and CASIA
both report corneal thickness, anterior chamber depth, lens thickness, white-to-white and posterior
corneal cylinder, on all 89 eyes and all 69 toric eyes. The earlier statement that the instrument
component could not be stacked was true of the 300-eye repeatability cohort and false of this data.

Running the same chain, the same eyes and the same outcomes, changing only where the posterior corneal
cylinder comes from:

| source of the posterior cylinder | residual cylinder | gain over the estimate | closer than the estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| IOLMaster | 0.2817 D | 0.3447 D | 50 of 69 |
| CASIA, aligned | 0.2934 D | 0.3330 D | 51 of 69 |
| population estimate | 0.6264 D | — | — |
| CASIA, axis as printed | 0.7577 D | −0.1313 D | 18 of 69 |

The two devices' *predictions* differ by 0.048 D on average, maximum 0.152 D. The improvement is
therefore a property of measuring the posterior cornea rather than of one device, which is a
considerably stronger statement than the original single-instrument result.

**The last row is mine and it is withdrawn.** Taking CASIA's posterior axis as printed gives 0.758 D,
worse than the population estimate, and read at face value that is a clean refutation of our own
headline. The two devices report orthogonal meridians: median axis difference 86°, 65 of 69 eyes between
75° and 105°, not one within 15°. The printed axis applies the cylinder 90° out. This is the same
axis-convention trap that once inflated this cell's residual to 5.2 D, and this time it was caught
before the number was reported rather than after.

The same error contaminated the first instrument measurement, which differenced signed cylinders across
the two conventions and reported 0.4263 D of device disagreement. Also withdrawn. Taken as power-vector
components with the conventions aligned, the real disagreement is 0.384 D in magnitude on average,
median 0.345, maximum 0.943 — and it is structured rather than random: a systematic −0.343 D in the
with/against-the-rule component and +0.028 D, i.e. nothing, in the oblique one.

That pair of components is the first error decomposition here that the graph engine's verifier certifies:
correlation −0.146 inside tolerance, `decorrelation_verified = True`, and a margin of 1.689 lying inside
the bracket 1.322 … 1.823 that the module computes for it. It passes the consistency check the withdrawn
toric decomposition failed, because neither component was constructed by subtraction from the total. The
Hill index flags a heavy tail, so a single standard deviation still understates the extremes.

## 11. The power decision made prospectively, which is the form in which it is usable

Replacing it with a prediction from preoperative data only — a leave-one-out linear fit on preoperative

## 12. A third independent source for the same input, and a model error it exposes

CASIA also measures the *total* corneal cylinder in one step, posterior included, where our chain
assembles it from an anterior surface plus a posterior surface. That makes a third independent source
for the input both decisions depend on.

| source of the corneal cylinder | residual cylinder |
|---|---|
| assembled from two surfaces, IOLMaster | 0.2817 D |
| CASIA posterior, aligned | 0.2934 D |
| CASIA total, measured in one step | 0.3112 D |
| population estimate | 0.6264 D |

Three sources that do not share an instrument path all land between 0.28 and 0.32 D against the
estimate's 0.63 D. The improvement is a property of measuring the cornea, not of any one device or of
how the surfaces are combined.

**The model error this exposes.** Our assembled cornea overstates the total corneal cylinder against a
direct measurement of the same quantity: 2.0291 D against 1.8314 D, mean absolute difference 0.2918 D.
That is a specific, located discrepancy in the corneal model rather than in the decision, and it is
larger than the residual the decision achieves.

**The convention was tested again, and the answer was the opposite.** Median axis gap between our
assembled cornea and CASIA's total is 3.55°, within 15° in 67 of 69 eyes and not one in the 75–105°
band — so here the two agree, unlike the posterior surface where they are orthogonal. Carrying the
posterior rule over to this quantity would have been wrong, which is the argument for measuring the
relationship each time rather than once.

One number in that cell is not evidence and is marked as such: the printed-axis and rotated-axis
residuals, 0.3112 and 0.3109 D, are identical because the toric lens is aligned to the combined corneal
axis, so rotating the cornea rotates the lens with it and the residual cannot change. The axis
agreement rests on the direct comparison alone.

## 13. The lens label is not exact, and its allowed error exceeds what our decision resolves

The power decision chooses between lenses on a 0.5 D manufacturing grid and treats the printed power as
exact. An external search returned the tolerance that standard actually permits — ISO 11979-2, in the
scheme that nine laboratories measuring the same lenses originally proposed:

| labelled power | allowed deviation |
|---|---|
| 0 to 15.00 D | ±0.30 D |
| 15.50 to 25.00 D | ±0.40 D |
| 25.50 to 30.00 D | ±0.50 D |
| above 30 D | ±1.00 D |

Applied to our own 89 eyes, where 68 lenses fall in the ±0.40 D band:

| | |
|---|---|
| mean allowed tolerance | ±0.401 D, a span of 0.80 D |
| our grid step | 0.50 D |
| eyes where the allowed tolerance alone exceeds half a grid step | **89 of 89** |
| our mean miss, prospective | 0.5345 D |
| eyes where our error is smaller than the lens's own allowed tolerance | 48 of 89 |

So the quantity the decision resolves is finer than the uncertainty in an input it treats as exact. In
more than half the cohort the lens is permitted to deviate by more than the distance between the two
powers we are choosing between.

Measured deviations from the same sources put the practical figure lower than the permitted one: 0.18 ±
0.12 D against an exact label, 0.23 ± 0.09 D against a label rounded to 0.5 D steps, and 0.24 ± 0.11 D
for powers at or above 20 D. Taking those at face value, the label could account for roughly a third of
our mean miss.

This is a floor rather than a correction. Knowing the label is uncertain does not let us choose better,
because we do not know which direction any individual lens deviates. What it does is bound what any
model can achieve from this input, and it says the next gain is not in the chain — it is in measuring
the implanted lens, or in choosing among lenses whose true power has been verified.

and are not independently confirmed here; what is measured here is what they imply for our 89 eyes.

## 14. The input uncertainty is the same size as our remaining error, so little headroom is left

Three measured floor terms, each known before the operation and none taken from the outcome — the lens
label (0.08 D below 20 D, 0.24 D at or above), the lens position prediction (0.1517 mm of spread times
each eye's own 1.3 D/mm sensitivity) and the between-device spread in mean corneal power (0.3610 D) —
compose to a floor on what any model can achieve from these inputs.

| | |
|---|---|
| floor, composed as independent | 0.4564 D mean, 0.4794 median |
| floor, composed as fully shared | 0.8057 D median |
| our realised mean miss, prospective | 0.5345 D |
| **the floor accounts for** | **85 % of our error** |
| the implanted lens on the same facit | 1.1304 D |
| headroom above the floor that existed | 0.6740 D |
| headroom we took | 0.5959 D |

So the chain is close to the best achievable from these measurements, and the comparison against the
implanted lens is not a narrow win over a weak control: the headroom was 0.67 D and 0.60 of it was
taken. The remaining model error is roughly 0.08 D, which is below the threshold anyone would act on.

A convention had to be separated from a disagreement to get this, for the third time in one day. The
two instruments differ on mean corneal power by +0.8511 D systematically, and that is the known gap
between keratometric power at an assumed index and true net corneal power including the posterior
surface — not a conflict. Using the offset instead of the spread would have inflated every eye's floor
by nearly a dioptre and made the budget overshoot the measured error.

**What this does not give, stated because it would be easy to claim.** The floor does not predict which
individual eye will miss: its correlation with the realised miss is +0.127. A threshold sweep produces a
difference of +0.179 D at a 0.45 D cut and differences of the opposite sign at 0.40 and 0.50, so that
figure is a threshold chosen on 89 eyes and not a signal. At the 0.25 D clinical threshold the floor
declines all 89 eyes, which means the honest output is a wider interval rather than a refusal.

**Correction to this section, found by auditing my own cell with the method the dental lane used on the
swarm: read the code behind the headline and check whether the claimed domain matches what is computed.**

I called the composed quantity a floor. It is not one. A floor must bound every eye, and 50 of 89 eyes
have a realised miss BELOW their own composed value — 0.2035 D against 0.4508 on average for that
group, while the 39 above average 0.9590 D. That is exactly what a composed standard deviation does: an
error lies below its own sigma roughly half the time. The median makes the same point and I missed it
first: median realised miss 0.3675 D against a median composed value of 0.4794 D, so the typical eye
beats the number I was calling a bound.

What survives, stated as what it is. The input uncertainties compose to 0.4564 D of the same kind of
quantity as the 0.5345 D mean error, which means the error we still carry is the size of the uncertainty
in what we feed the chain. That is a statement about headroom, not a proof of irreducibility: the
remaining model error cannot be read off as 0.08 D, because the two numbers are not a bound and a
measurement but two magnitudes of the same order. The comparison against the implanted lens at 1.1304 D
is unaffected, since both are measured against the same facit.

## 15. A prediction beat a measurement once, and asking where else turned it into a rule

Feeding the chain a predicted lens position beat feeding it the position measured after surgery, 0.5345
against 0.5690 D. If that is simply noise smoothing it should generalise, so it was tested on the four
quantities where two independent instruments measure the same 89 eyes — which makes the measurement's
disagreement with itself observed rather than assumed.

| quantity | the two instruments disagree by | prediction from the other quantities misses by | cohort mean control |
|---|---|---|---|
| corneal thickness | **9.91 µm** | 28.51 | 30.70 |
| anterior chamber depth | **0.155 mm** | 0.178 | 0.325 |
| lens thickness | **0.041 mm** | 0.285 | 0.388 |
| white-to-white | **0.162 mm** | 0.244 | 0.334 |

It does not generalise. The instrument wins for all four, by a factor of seven for lens thickness. The
predictions are genuine — each beats the leave-one-out cohort mean — but none beats its instrument, so
these quantities carry information that no regularity among the others supplies.

**The negative is what makes the principle usable.** A prediction can beat a measurement when the
measurement is taken in a *different state* than the one the decision needs. The lens position is
measured after surgery, so its single measurement carries surgical and healing variability, while the
prediction targets the expected position of the state the decision concerns. The four quantities above
are measured on a stable preoperative eye, the same state the chain consumes, and there the instrument
is unbeatable.

That is falsifiable and the refutation is specific: find a quantity measured in the same state as the
decision where a prediction still beats the instrument. That would mean the state argument is wrong and
the cause is something else, most likely that the chain amplifies the quantity more than its own noise.

## 16. A decision outside the eye: two calibration errors that look like they cancel, compound

Every decision so far was in the eye, which I had taken for a property of the project. It is a property
of where I looked: the constraint net holds twelve edges outside the eye carrying an external reference
against three inside it. The laser line has the sharpest of them, because its anchor is a published
*law* rather than a single value — a mass-loss regression with both coefficients held out.

| | threshold | slope |
|---|---|---|
| published, held out | 1.15 J/cm² | 267 µg/J |
| our chain | 1.4548 J/cm² | 200.33 µg/J |
| relative error | **+26.5 %** | **−25.0 %** |

Reported that way the two look as though they might offset. Inverted into a decision — choose the
fluence that removes a target mass, then ask the published law what that fluence actually removes —
they compound, because a threshold set too high asks for more fluence and a slope set too low asks for
more again:

| target µg/cm² | fluence we would choose | published law would choose | actually removed | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.58 | 1.24 | 114.7 | **+359 %** |
| 100 | 1.95 | 1.52 | 214.7 | +115 % |
| 400 | 3.45 | 2.65 | 614.5 | +54 % |
| 800 | 5.45 | 4.15 | 1147.6 | +43 % |

The error is largest for the smallest targets, which is where conservative removal matters most. Against
the other control — one fixed fluence, as a device setting gives — our rule is better at four of the six
targets and worse at two, so it beats a constant but not by much.

**The transferable part is the reporting.** Two relative errors on the coefficients of a law do not tell
you what the law costs as a decision, and can suggest the opposite of the truth. This pair was sitting
in a round report as "+26.5 % and −25.0 %" and had been read as partially self-cancelling.

A convention is in the way and is carried rather than resolved: the same lane computes the threshold as
1.4548 J/cm² for a Gaussian beam proxy and 1.9081 for a top-hat, a 31 % spread from the beam profile
alone — larger than the slope error. Both are in the output.

Scope: scored against a published law, not against measured tissue outcomes. No biological or clinical
validity is claimed.

## 17. Correction: the 0.5345 D is in the lens plane, not the spectacle plane

The lane built the error budget in the spectacle plane and recorded one line that corrects every
comparison I made tonight: `original_0_5345D_is = "IOL label-plane action MAE, not spectacle-plane
refraction MAE"`.

The decision chooses a lens power, so its error is in dioptres of *lens label*. The uncertainty terms I
composed against it — device disagreement, position prediction, label tolerance — are partly in dioptres
of *refraction at the spectacle plane*. Those are different units, related by a measured sensitivity of
0.6864 D per D (SD 0.018 across 89 eyes). I compared across planes.

Converted consistently:

| | lens plane | spectacle plane |
|---|---|---|
| our prospective decision | 0.5345 D | **0.3669 D** |
| the implanted lens | 1.1304 D | **0.7759 D** |

And the budget composed in the spectacle plane, with the measured 0.18 D label deviation: 0.3672 D by
quadrature. That is the same number as the converted measurement to four digits, so the budget closes —
but it closes in the spectacle plane, which is not the comparison I published.

Two further results from the same round, both verified against its own output file:

**The terms partially cancel.** Paired covariance on the net corneal path gives 0.4162 D by quadrature
and 0.5725 D summed, while the *measured* row sum is 0.3728 D — tighter than quadrature. Negative
covariance between the terms, which is real information and the opposite of the conservative assumption.

**The keratometric offset is exactly a convention.** The inferred index delta is 0.3375 for all 89 eyes
with a standard deviation of 1.1e-16, i.e. identical to machine precision. That settles what I had read
from the +0.8511 D mean offset: it is the assumed keratometric index, not an instrument disagreement.
The usable disagreement term is the net one, mean 0.2302 D with 54 of 89 eyes inside 0.25 D.

The round gates UNKNOWN and its stated obstacle is matched signed label, axial-length and corneal
calibration errors, which do not exist in our data.

## 18. A second decision outside the eye, and this one the twin can answer

A capability brief asked the complement cell what it could decide that nobody had asked it to. It
returned an inverse question with four named external anchors and its own falsification conditions
written in advance: at what host-side recycle fraction does the model flip from "the host is lysed" to
"the host is spared while the activator is still lysed", and is the flip a point?

Run on the cell itself, both arms swept independently:

| arm | growth at zero recycling | at full recycling | flip | sign changes over 2001 points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| activator | +0.2109 /min | +0.2199 /min | **never flips** | 0 |
| host | −0.1511 /min | +0.0553 /min | **0.69012** | 1 |

So the answer is yes and the window is one-sided: the host is spared for every recycle fraction below
0.69012 while the activator is lysed across the whole range. Just below the flip the host sits at
−0.000188 /min against the activator's +0.2171 /min — a separation of three orders of magnitude in the
same model with only the surface changed, which is the equally informed control.

The flip is a point and not a plateau: one sign change in 2001 samples. The 0.69012 reproduces the
value already standing on a TIGHT edge of the constraint net, so the cell and the net agree.

**The constraint the brief found, which is the night's recurring shape for the fourth time.** The
recycle branch requires a partner — C3b, C3(H₂O) or CVF — and nothing in the cell records which. The
flip fraction is therefore conditional on a branch label the model does not carry, exactly as the
posterior corneal axis was conditional on a meridian convention and the keratometric offset on an
assumed index. Inverting with the wrong branch gives a confidently wrong number.

Two of the three pre-registered falsifiers could not fire, and that is itself informative: both arms
flipping at the same fraction is impossible when one never flips, and the plateau condition is ruled out
by the single crossing. The third, on discriminating power, is not tested here.

One correction of my own: the first version of this cell required *both* arms to flip and reported no
window. That was my framing, not the result — the activator never flipping is precisely why a window
exists.

Scope: algebraic behaviour of one supplied model under a swept parameter. No biological or clinical
validity, and the branch label is unresolved.
