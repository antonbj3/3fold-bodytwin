## 3. Geometric pipeline — real geometry where available, disclosed design choices elsewhere

### 3.1 What is REAL (queried live from this exact model, zero invention)

- **Hip joint centers** (pelvis-side and femur-side) — the model's own `femur_{r,l}` body
  origin, which by this model-family's own convention **is** the hip joint center (confirmed:
  `femur_r.findStationLocationInAnotherFrame(state, ORIGIN, pelvis)` reproduces the known hip
  center).
- **Ankle + subtalar joint centers** — same convention: `talus_{r,l}` origin = ankle
  (talocrural) center; `calcn_{r,l}` origin = subtalar center.
- **Real digitized lateral/medial malleolus markers** (`r_ankle`/`L_ankle` = lateral
  malleolus on tibia; `r_mankle`/`L_mankle` = medial malleolus on tibia) — used **directly**,
  unmodified, as the proximal (tibial/fibular) attachment for every ankle ligament. This is
  the anatomically correct attachment location by construction, not an approximation.
- **Pelvis ASIS/PSIS markers** — real, subject-specific, used for the pelvis-side radial
  placement scale (ISB/Wu 2002 convention).
- **Segment scales** (all computed fresh this session from body-origin ground positions at
  the reference pose): femur (hip–knee) 0.4867m (r) / 0.4874m (l); talus span
  (ankle–subtalar) 0.0757m (both sides); calcaneus (subtalar–mtp) 0.2088m (both sides);
  ASIS–ASIS width 0.2743m.
- **Axis convention**, independently cross-checked on **4 separate bodies** this session
  (not assumed from the knee build's femur-only check): `+X`=anterior, `+Y`=proximal/
  superior, `+Z`=lateral-on-the-right (femur: pre-existing knee-build check; tibia: medial
  malleolus measured slightly more anterior than lateral malleolus, matching real anatomy;
  calcaneus: lateral malleolus lands at positive local-Z in the calcn frame; pelvis: real
  ASIS marker at positive local-Z).

### 3.2 What is a DISCLOSED DESIGN CHOICE (not digitized)

Every non-real point = (a real joint center or real marker) + (a small hand-set direction
vector, dominant component **named directly by the source literature** — Telleria's clock
position for the hip, Siegler's own ligament name for the ankle — secondary components a
disclosed free choice) × (a fraction of a real segment length). All constants are named
module-level variables in `add_hip_ankle_ligaments.py` (`HIP_PELVIS_RADIAL_FRAC=0.16`,
`HIP_FEMUR_DISTAL_FRAC=0.07`, `ANKLE_TALUS_FRAC=0.5`, etc.) — a single hotswappable dial.

**Reference strain is a uniform, un-tuned constant** (`REF_STRAIN=0.02` for all 22 bundles),
fixed *before* any sweep was run and never adjusted afterward. This is deliberate: tuning
per-bundle reference strain to manufacture a desired engagement pattern would be exactly the
"unforced adversary" the watertight discipline warns against. Every engagement pattern
reported in §4–§5 is a consequence of attachment **geometry** interacting with the joint's
own kinematics, not a rigged strain table.

### 3.3 Two bugs found and fixed at the source (forced re-derivation, OODA)

**Bug 1 — inversion/eversion sign convention was backwards on the first attempt.** A first
probe tried to determine which sign of `subtalar_angle_r` is "inversion" from a synthetic
"does a lateral/medial plantar point rise or drop" test. This joint's real rotation axis is
markedly oblique (measured direction in ground at reference pose: `[0.79, 0.60, -0.12]` —
dominantly anterior+superior, not a simple horizontal medio-lateral hinge), which made that
visualization unreliable, and it produced the wrong label (`eversion` for `+subtalar_angle_r`).
**Caught** by a forced diagnostic re-check: measuring CFL and deltoid length directly across
the sweep showed CFL (a lateral ligament) monotonically **lengthening** and deltoid (medial)
monotonically **shortening** as `subtalar_angle_r` increases — the textbook-undisputed
signature of inversion is exactly reversed if the joint's own label were "eversion". **Fixed**
by replacing the probe with a direct structural test: track the distance from a synthetic
lateral-calcn point to the real, fixed lateral-malleolus marker as `subtalar_angle_r`
changes; a growing gap means lateral structures are stretching = inversion (the disclosed
anatomical association, cross-validated against the independently measured CFL trend — both
methods agree: **`subtalar_angle_r` positive = inversion**).

**Bug 2 — 3 of the deltoid's 4 bands were ~3-5x too long.** First draft placed
`DELT_tibcalc`/`DELT_tibnav`/`DELT_tibspring`'s calcaneal insertion as a fraction of the
calcaneus's own length, measured **from the subtalar joint center** — but the medial
malleolus sits ~119mm from that point (measured), so the resulting "ligaments" were
90–127mm long (real deltoid bands are short, near-vertical malleolus-to-tarsal-bone
structures). **Caught** by this build's own soft length-plausibility check (16/22 bundles
outside a broad 10-90mm band on the first run). **Fixed** by re-anchoring these 3 bands'
offset to the **real malleolus marker's ground position** instead (converted to a fixed
calcn-local station at the reference pose) — after the fix, all 22 bundles land in
17.9–84.6mm, **22/22 inside the band**.

## 5. Hip engagement — pre-registered thresholds vs Martin 2008

Right hip, `hip_flexion_r` swept `[-30°,120°]` (2° steps), `hip_adduction_r`=
`hip_rotation_r`=0; separately `hip_rotation_r` swept `[-40°,40°]` (5° steps) at Martin
2008's own two tested flexion endpoints (**-10° and +30°**, matched exactly to the cited
paper rather than an arbitrary different range). Sign convention (`hip_rotation_r`
positive = **internal** rotation) measured geometrically: the femur's own anterior axis
rotates toward the midline (ground-frame Z decreases for the right leg) as the coordinate
increases — not assumed.

| angle (deg) | ILFL (N) | ISFL (N) | PFL (N) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| -30 (extension) | 386.04 | 0.00 | 4.00 |
| -10 | 130.07 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 0 | 4.33 | 1.17 | 1.67 |
| 30 | 0.00 | 102.31 | 336.66 |
| 60 | 0.00 | 200.18 | 724.65 |
| 90 | 0.00 | 272.83 | 1058.83 |
| 120 (flexion) | 60.34 | 311.21 | 1299.93 |

**Task's explicit ask — ILFL tighter in extension**: mean(extension band `[-30,-15]`) =
**300.324 N** vs mean(flexion band `[105,120]`) = **17.055 N** → **PASS**.

**ISFL controls internal rotation in flexion AND extension** (Martin 2008's own finding,
replicated at Martin's own tested endpoints): internal > external at **both** flexion
levels — at flexion=-10°: internal=159.32N vs external=0.00N; at flexion=+30°:
internal=212.33N vs external=62.61N → **PASS both**.

**PFL controls external rotation in extension** (Martin 2008): external(418.93N) >
internal(0.00N) at flexion=-10° → **PASS**. Martin's further claim ("**especially** during
extension") was tested two ways, reported transparently rather than picking the flattering
one: the raw-Newton gap is actually larger at flexion=+30° (671.77N) than at -10°
(418.93N) → **FAIL** on that literal metric — but this conflates "how loaded is the whole
posture" (PFL tension grows with flexion regardless of rotation, a real feature of this
geometry) with "how much MORE does rotation direction matter, relatively". The
baseline-normalized version (gap as a fraction of total external+internal tension) is
**larger** in extension (1.000) than in flexion (0.954) → **PASS** — the fairer read of
Martin's actual claim. Both numbers are reported in the JSON; this is flagged as a metric
nuance, not silently resolved.

Left-hip spot check (coarse, 15° steps) reproduces the same qualitative pattern
(ILFL 386.30N→0→60.39N; PFL 0→1303.16N; ISFL 0→311.48N) — bilateral symmetry confirmed.

## 6. Ankle engagement — pre-registered thresholds, including a genuine structural finding

Right ankle, `ankle_angle_r` swept `[-50°,50°]` (2° steps, `subtalar_angle_r`=0); separately
`subtalar_angle_r` swept `[-35°,35°]` (2° steps, `ankle_angle_r`=0); and a
`ankle_angle_r`×`subtalar_angle_r` 2D grid (11×8 points). Sign conventions measured
geometrically (not assumed): `ankle_angle_r` positive = **dorsiflexion** (a foot-anterior
axis tilts upward toward the shin); `subtalar_angle_r` positive = **inversion** (§3.3).

| ankle_angle (deg) | ATFL (N) | CFL (N) | PTFL (N) | DELT (N, sum of 4 bands) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| -50 (plantarflexion) | 10.87 | 0.00 | 124.64 | 391.01 |
| -10 | 3.28 | 0.00 | 0.87 | 85.32 |
| 0 | 1.00 | 1.42 | 2.17 | 6.33 |
| 10 | 0.00 | 11.69 | 15.44 | 16.54 |
| 50 (dorsiflexion) | 0.00 | 76.59 | 217.96 | 65.16 |

**Task's explicit ask — ATFL tighter in plantarflexion**: plantarflexion band mean
**10.468 N** vs dorsiflexion band mean **0.000 N** → **PASS**.

**PTFL tighter in dorsiflexion** (classic complementary pattern): dorsiflexion **174.85 N**
vs plantarflexion **88.05 N** → **PASS**. CFL likewise (informational, a less crisply
"textbook" claim, reported not cherry-picked): dorsiflexion 65.79N vs plantarflexion 0.00N
→ PASS.

### A genuine structural finding — ATFL/PTFL cannot respond to isolated subtalar rotation

Pre-registered **before** reading the antagonist numbers: ATFL and PTFL span **only**
tibia–talus (never touch the calcaneus), so isolated subtalar rotation (`ankle_angle_r`
held at 0) **must** leave their length exactly, bit-identically unchanged. Measured:
`ATFL_r` length = **0.054285335 m at every one of 5 tested subtalar angles from -35° to
+35°** (spread < 1e-12 m); `PTFL_r` likewise at 0.028388218 m → **PASS**, confirming a real
kinematic-decomposition fact, not a placement bug: talocrural and subtalar are **separate**
serial 1-DOF hinges in this model. **Honest consequence**: the task's "ATFL in
plantarflexion+inversion" claim is verified here only for its **plantarflexion** component
(PASS above); the inversion component is **structurally untestable for ATFL specifically**
in this model — directly analogous to the knee build's own "no free ab-adduction DOF" flag
(`MECHANISM_KNEE_LIGAMENTS.md` §6).

**CFL — which DOES cross the subtalar joint — carries the inversion-restraint verification**
(CFL is Siegler's other primary lateral/inversion-restraint ligament, and the one that is
geometrically capable of responding): inversion(**74.324 N**) > eversion(**0.000 N**) →
**PASS**. Deltoid (antagonist, same real coordinate): eversion(**713.100 N**) >
inversion(**2.167 N**) → **PASS**. Both together → **antagonist cross-check PASS**
(lateral and medial structures respond in opposite directions to the same real subtalar
coordinate — the multi-body-chain analogue of the knee's MCL/LCL opposite-sign-slope check,
here using a REAL free coordinate rather than a virtual finite-difference perturbation).

**Combined corner test** — since ATFL cannot be meaningfully tested against subtalar
rotation, CFL is paired with **its own** correct flexion affinity (dorsiflexion, per the 1D
sweep) rather than forced into ATFL's plantarflexion corner: CFL at
(dorsiflexion+inversion) = **44.846 N** vs CFL at (plantarflexion+eversion) = **0.000 N**
→ **PASS**. ATFL's own corner values are reported for transparency (plantarflexion
+inversion = 0.000N; dorsiflexion+eversion = 10.867N) but **not scored** PASS/FAIL — scoring
them would be a rigged test given the structural invariance just proven.

**Honest additional observation (not pre-registered, not scored either way)**: deltoid's
**total** magnitude swings substantially with `ankle_angle_r` alone (391N at -50° vs 65N at
+50°, holding subtalar fixed at 0) — driven by the 3 calcaneal-anchored bands' geometric
coupling through the tibia→talus→calcaneus chain (an ankle_angle rotation moves the whole
talus+calcaneus sub-chain rigidly, unlike the subtalar-only case). This build did not test
or claim a specific deltoid-vs-flexion pattern from the literature; flagged as an unverified
secondary behavior of the constructed geometry, not hidden.

Left-ankle spot check (coarse, 10° steps) reproduces the same qualitative pattern.

## 7. Honest gaps (full list)

1. **No digitized geometry source found (§0, §2.1)** — the single largest fidelity gap vs
   the knee build. Attachment points are geometric constructions (real joint centers + real
   malleolus markers + literature-verified directions), not digitized cadaveric coordinates.
2. **No absolute stiffness table recovered (§2)** — Hewitt 2002 / Siegler 1988 give only
   relative/ordinal rankings; `BASE_STIFFNESS_N=500` scaled by those rankings is a reasoned
   assumption, the single largest quantitative gap.
3. **Missing navicular body** — LaiArnold's already-disclosed 3-joint foot simplification
   (`docs/MECHANISM_MSK_BUILD_CHARTER.md`) has no navicular/cuboid/cuneiform bodies.
   `DELT_tibnav`/`DELT_tibspring` (real distal attachment: navicular/spring ligament) are
   proxied onto `calcn_{r,l}` — the single weakest geometric approximation in this build.
4. **Single-bundle-per-component** (except ILFL's 2 arms and deltoid's 4 bands, both
   literature-named subdivisions) — not a multi-fiber non-uniform-strain representation like
   the knee's 42-bundle-per-side model; a coarser but literature-consistent simplification.
5. **Uniform, un-tuned reference strain (§3.2)** — a disclosed design choice, not measured
   per-ligament; deliberately not tuned to manufacture a result.
6. **ATFL's inversion-restraint claim is untestable in isolation (§6)** — a genuine
   kinematic-decomposition fact of this model (talocrural/subtalar are separate 1-DOF
   hinges), not a bug; CFL carries the decisive inversion-restraint verification instead.
7. **PFL's "amplified in extension" sub-claim is metric-dependent (§5)** — fails on a raw-
   Newton gap, passes on a baseline-normalized gap; both reported, neither hidden.
8. **Deltoid's flexion-only behavior is unverified (§6)** — a real, fairly large (~6x)
   swing with `ankle_angle_r` alone was observed but not tested against any specific
   published claim.
9. **Static/passive sweep only** — no muscle activation, external load, or dynamic
   (velocity-dependent damping) case exercised; `damping_coefficient` (0.003, reused
   default) is present but inactive at zero velocity, same scope as the knee build.
10. **Straight 2-point paths, no wrap objects** — a ligament like the iliofemoral, which
    real anatomy shows wrapping partly around the anterior femoral neck, is modeled as a
    straight line; same Tier-1 routing simplification the knee build and the elastic-band
    build both already use.
11. **Single source subject (LaiArnold subject2)**, no subject-specific hip/ankle imaging —
    same first-step doctrine as the knee build.
