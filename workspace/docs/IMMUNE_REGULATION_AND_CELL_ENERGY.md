# Immune regulation and cell energy

Do complement-growth calculations identify host protection, and does shared pore involvement predict the benefit of combined cardioprotection?
They do not: growth has no calibrated link to injury or clearance, and single-treatment infarct measurements leave the combination outcome undetermined.

No measured coupling connects these complement results to ATP production or to intestinal hydrogen output. Underactivation and overactivation require separate activator-clearance and host-injury observations. [8]

## Complement amplification is not a protection measurement

Published enzyme kinetics give conditional cleavage counts. The surface substrate concentration and the effective yield of new convertases are unmeasured, so neither the half-saturation nor the saturation scenario establishes the actual amplification rate. [1, 2]

| Quantity or test | Inputs or comparison | Output and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Convertase kinetics [1] | Turnover 1.78 per second; half-saturation constant 5.86e-06 M; surface/fluid half-lives 90/77 s | Published inputs; surface substrate concentration is absent. |
| Cleavages per surface convertase lifetime [1] | Substrate at the Michaelis constant / saturation | 115.5599 / 231.1197; these are scenarios, not a bracket for every substrate concentration. |
| Effective new-convertase yield per cleavage [1] | Break-even reciprocal of those cleavage counts | Critical fractions 0.008654 / 0.004327; actual yield is unmeasured. |
| Decay-only regulation [2] | Activity suppression factor 100 | An activity ratio and a cleavage count cannot alone establish quiescence or runaway. |

The scenarios do not bound every substrate concentration, including the low-substrate regime. An activity-suppression ratio and a cleavage count are also different quantities, so their comparison cannot establish quiescence or runaway. [1, 2]

The recycling windows describe growth within a calculation. Recycling itself is an unmeasured assumption, and the boundary has no calibrated connection to lysis, host injury or pathogen clearance. [8]

|---|---|---|
| Recycling growth-sign window [8] | rho interval [0, 0.690115014) | Declining host amplification inside the interval; the activator remains growing. Lysis is not measured. |

The absolute rate ratio grows as host growth approaches zero. A large ratio therefore need not imply effective defence, and the headline discrimination values do not use the same definition as the signed rate endpoints. [8]

The failed forecast and membrane attack complex (MAC) counterexample prevent inferring terminal response from the growth summaries. Synthetic terminal quotas provide no measured lower boundary for underactivation. [8]

## Tubular membrane face: secretion, not deposition

Human tubular-cell supernatants show greater apical C3 secretion at rest. The same comparison under luminal transferrin does not name a face under its stated cutoff. These are secretion measurements, not measurements of surface deposition or injury. [3]

| Quantity or test | Inputs or comparison | Output and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Polarised C3 secretion at rest [3] | Basolateral/apical ratio 0.45 +/- 0.16 | 3.4375 quoted dispersions from symmetry; greater apical secretion, mislabelled as deposition in the decision output. |
| Polarised C3 secretion with luminal transferrin [3] | Ratio 0.93 +/- 0.24 | 0.2917 dispersions from symmetry, below its 2 cutoff: no face named. |
| Rest-to-transferrin secretion shift [3, 8] | Shift 0.4800; combined quoted dispersion 0.2884 | Separation 1.6641 dispersion units |

The quoted dispersions have unresolved type and pairing. Distances from symmetry are conditional arithmetic, not independent significance tests. Rodent regulator localisation does not calibrate human surface deposition or transfer the secretion contrast into a risk estimate. [3, 8]

## Shared pore involvement does not fix a combination ceiling

The mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) comparison concerns rabbit infarct percentage of anatomical area at risk (AAR). It does not measure ATP flux. The exact combination endpoint is unknown, and the lowest published single-arm mean is not a physical floor. [4, 8]

| Quantity or test | Inputs or comparison | Output and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Rabbit infarct source arms [4] | Control 61 +/- 6%; postconditioning 29 +/- 4% | NIM811 20 +/- 4%; preconditioning benchmark 18 +/- 4%; all AAR. |
| Unmeasured combination [4, 8] | Best-single ceiling / independent residual-injury product | 20% / 9.5082% AAR; gap 10.4918 pp. |
| Dispersion assumed in the decision [8] | Assumed SD 4 pp; hypothetical SEM with n = 7 | Separations 2.6230 / 0.9914 SD; the latter uses the wrong cohort for infarct. |
| Dispersion under the source convention [8] | SEM, infarct n = 10 | Treated SD 12.6491 pp; separation 0.8294 SD. Existing singles do not decide a shared ceiling. |
| Source-mean uncertainty [8] | Independent [1.8160, 29.7834]%; ceiling [8.2667, 31.7333]% AAR | Paired gap [0.5080, 18.9216] pp under the conditional normal-sampling reference box; not an empirical combination interval. |
| Identical infarct marginals [8] | Noninterfering union of rescued tissue parcels | Combination can span [0, 20]% AAR; shared pore involvement does not fix rescue overlap. |
| Transferred best-single ceiling [5] | Different drug/protocol pair: predicted 43% AAR | Measured 27 +/- 4%; residual -16 pp. Portable rule fails; not the exact target pair. |

The source convention is SEM, with the infarct cohort distinct from the mitochondrial cohort. The uncertainty intervals are conditional calculations from a normal-sampling reference box, not empirical combination intervals. Fractions are dimensionless, and pp means percentage points. [8]

Identical single-treatment infarct marginals permit different overlaps in rescued tissue. Shared pore involvement therefore does not determine combination benefit, pathway identity or mitochondrial ATP supply. The external failure concerns a different drug/protocol pair and does not measure the target combination. [4, 5, 8]

| Combination comparison [8] | Recorded rejection rule | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Shared-ceiling midpoint | Reject at or below 14.7541% AAR | Constraint record instead says at or above the same midpoint |
| Outcome above the postconditioning arm | Above 29% rejects both simple readings | Endpoint and uncertainty must be scored together |

## Expelled hydrogen does not uniquely identify production

The hydrogen calculation combines absorption and daily flatus records from different protocols. Its regime threshold concerns colonic accumulation in the source, but expelled output in the calculation. That mismatch leaves regime identity unresolved. [6, 7]

| Quantity or test | Inputs or comparison | Output and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen regime threshold [6] | Absorption endpoints 20–90%; threshold 76 mL/6h | The source threshold concerns colonic accumulation; the script applies it to expelled output. Regime identity is unresolved. |
| Gas scenario below the script threshold [6] | Output 60 mL/6h; fixed-fraction estimate 133.3 | Production >= 75 mL/6h with no finite upper bound; arithmetic conditional on that threshold. |
| Gas scenario above the script threshold [6] | Output 100 mL/6h; fixed-fraction estimate 222.2 | Production [125, 1000] mL/6h; no measured production score. |
| Gas “mass closure” [7] | Daily medians: total 705, hydrogen 361, carbon dioxide 68, residual 213 mL | Arithmetic remainder 63 mL/day; subtracting separate medians does not establish missing gas in any subject. |

The production intervals remain scenarios until accumulation, expelled hydrogen and absorbed hydrogen are distinguished. There is no measured production score. [6]

Subtracting separate daily medians does not demonstrate missing gas in any subject. Conservation requires paired species volumes, just as a production comparison requires paired production, breath and expelled-hydrogen observations. [6, 7]
